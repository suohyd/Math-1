Note 1.1.13.

If inverse of the matrix exists then it is unique. Indeed, if \(B,C\in M_n(\mathbb{K})\) are two inverses of \(A\in M_n(\mathbb{K})\) then we have
\begin{equation*} B=BI_n=B(AC)=(BA)C=I_nC=C. \end{equation*}
🔗
Note that if \(A\in M_n(\mathbb{K})\) is invertible then the inverse of \(A^{-1}\in M_n(\mathbb{K})\) is \(A\text{,}\) i.e.,
\begin{equation*} \left(A^{-1}\right)^{-1}=A. \end{equation*}
🔗
If \(A,B\in M_n(\K)\) are invertible then \(AB\in M_n(\K)\) is also invertible and
\begin{equation*} (AB)^{-1}=B^{-1}A^{-1}. \end{equation*}
